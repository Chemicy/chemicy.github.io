A Field Guide to Obvious Theorems 🫠

Nine calculus theorems that look terribly impressive in textbooks. Once you truly understand them, you'll feel a very particular kind of beauty, the kind a Chinese internet joke sums up as: "Listening to you talk was exactly like listening to you talk."

🧑‍🌾 A tip from the village chief: Draw any curve on the grid paper, then draw a dashed horizontal line at a height between the curve's two ends. Whoa! The curve is guaranteed to touch it. How amazing. Congratulations, you've just independently discovered the Intermediate Value Theorem.

🌳 How the nine obvious statements are related

🌳 Nine obvious statements
│
├── ✏️ Only need "drawn in one stroke" (continuous)
│   ├── Zero Theorem
│   ├── Intermediate Value    ← the Zero Theorem with the line raised
│   └── Extreme Value
│
├── 🤏 About "where it ends up" (limits)
│   ├── Squeeze Theorem
│   └── Monotone Convergence
│
└── 🛹 Also need "no sharp corners" (differentiable)
    ├── Fermat's Lemma
    ├── Rolle's Theorem    ← Extreme Value + Fermat
    ├── Lagrange           ← Rolle with your head tilted
    └── Cauchy             ← Lagrange driving two cars

🤔 So why do mathematicians bother writing down the obvious?

Because "you can see it in the picture" doesn't count. You drew that picture, and there are plenty of weird curves you could never draw. Each of those wordy conditions at the start of a theorem plugs a real loophole, and every "When the obvious breaks" box shows you what that loophole looks like.

That's why exams always test the conditions: closed interval or open, continuous or differentiable. You can guess the conclusion with your eyes closed. It's obvious, after all.
